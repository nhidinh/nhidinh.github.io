## Introduction

Automation adoption in software projects is often discussed as a technical challenge — choosing the right tools, building frameworks, or designing stable test suites.

In practice, however, automation adoption is often a **people challenge**.

Manual testers may be interested in automation but lack the opportunity to learn it in a practical environment. At the same time, automation engineers are often focused on project delivery rather than knowledge sharing.

In early 2024, we organized an internal **Automation Training Program** aimed at bridging this gap by introducing manual testers to automation concepts and practical usage within real projects.

The goal was not to transform participants into full automation engineers immediately, but to provide a foundation that would allow them to gradually participate in automation activities.

---

## Program Overview

The program ran for approximately **three months**, from March to June 2024.

More than **twenty manual testers** participated, supported by several automation engineers who volunteered as mentors and presenters.

Sessions were conducted through a combination of:

- online training
- recorded sessions
- in-person workshops

Rather than focusing purely on theoretical concepts, the program was designed around **practical exposure to real automation workflows used in our projects**.

---

## Training Focus

The program focused on two main areas.

### Automation Fundamentals

The first part introduced the foundational concepts of automation testing, including:

- the role of automation in modern software projects  
- common automation strategies  
- typical tools and technologies used in automation testing  

The intention was to help participants understand **why automation exists**, not just how to write test scripts.

---

### Applying Automation in Real Projects

The second part focused on practical exposure to the automation frameworks currently running in our projects.

Participants were guided through:

- understanding the structure of automation frameworks  
- executing regression tests  
- analyzing test results  
- debugging issues using development tools  
- writing simple automation scripts  

Additional sessions also introduced supporting engineering practices such as version control and CI/CD workflows.

These topics were included because automation rarely exists in isolation — it is part of a broader engineering ecosystem.

---

## Building a Testing Community

One of the most valuable outcomes of the program was the collaboration it created within the department.

Automation engineers from different projects volunteered to share their experience and contribute as mentors. Over time, the sessions evolved beyond simple lectures into open discussions where testers could exchange ideas about testing practices and automation approaches.

In many ways, the program unintentionally helped form a small internal **testing community**, where both manual testers and automation engineers could learn from each other.

For presenters, the experience was also valuable. Teaching a topic often reinforces understanding and encourages engineers to reflect more deeply on their own practices.

---

## Challenges and Lessons Learned

Like any internal initiative, the program was not without challenges.

### Presentation Quality

Since multiple engineers contributed as presenters, the quality of sessions naturally varied.

Some presenters were highly effective at explaining technical topics clearly and concisely, while others were less experienced with teaching or presenting.

This highlighted the importance of supporting presenters with preparation guidelines or structured materials in future programs.

---

### Maintaining Participation

Maintaining consistent participation was another challenge.

Most participants were working on active projects, and their primary responsibility remained project delivery rather than training. As a result, the program schedule had to remain flexible and spread across several months to accommodate project workloads.

Balancing learning with delivery responsibilities remains one of the biggest challenges for internal training initiatives.

---

## Outcomes

Although the program was not designed to produce immediate large-scale changes, it did produce several positive outcomes.

Most notably, **three participants were able to transition into hybrid roles**, contributing to automation tasks within their projects.

Their involvement was still at an early stage, primarily focused on scripting tasks, but it represented an important step forward in expanding automation participation and opening new career paths for manual testers.

---

## Final Thoughts

Automation adoption is not only about tools or frameworks.

It is also about **knowledge distribution**.

Building automation systems is important, but enabling teams to understand and participate in those systems is equally critical.

This training program was a small step toward bridging that gap. While the results were modest, it demonstrated that creating opportunities for learning and knowledge sharing can gradually expand automation capabilities across a team.
